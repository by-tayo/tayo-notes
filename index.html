I imagine life after graduation feeling a little like the summer days of high school, when there was no school to worry about. I look forward to having more freedom to enjoy life and experience the world around me.

How am I feeling today?

Today, I feel somewhat happy, somewhat sad, and low on energy. If I had to name it, I’d call it bittersweet, a mixture of emotions as I try to manage everything going on in my life.

One thing that has been on my mind lately is how important financial decisions have become. The choices I make today can shape my future, so I want to be more thoughtful about how I manage my money and plan for the life I want.

02

What did I experience today?

I’m preparing to transition into the vulnerability management area with the tooling team. I also recently interviewed with a higher education institution for a cybersecurity co-op.

During the interview, I noticed that many of the questions focused on software development, DevOps, and cloud technologies. These are areas I’m familiar with, and the conversation reminded me of the work experiences I’ve had in the past.

It felt good to revisit those topics. Sometimes an opportunity reconnects me with an interest I stepped away from for a while. Whether I’m reconnecting with someone from my past or sitting in an interview, moments like these remind me that I can return to something after a hiatus and find my way forward again.

03

What kind of friendships do I want to build?

I want to build friendships with people who understand that I have flaws, just as they do. I value honesty because the truth helps me understand who I am and where I might need to change.

I want friendships where we can be honest with each other without tearing one another down. I want to be able to accept constructive feedback, recognize my mistakes, and grow into a better version of myself.

I also understand that building friendships takes effort. I have to approach people and give relationships a chance to develop, even when it feels uncomfortable.

04

What am I learning about myself?

My past experiences have shaped how I approach trust. They’ve taught me to be cautious about letting people get close. They’ve also taught me to notice when someone crosses my boundaries or turns a small disagreement into something bigger.

At the same time, I know I can’t build meaningful friendships if I never give people a chance. Everyone has their own responsibilities, and people get busy. Not everyone will connect with me, and that’s okay.

I’m learning that I can protect my boundaries without closing myself off completely.

I’m also learning that it’s important to balance ambition with enjoying life. I want to work toward my goals, but I also need time to relax, decompress, and enjoy the things that make me who I am.

05

What do I want to remember?

I want to remember that I need to slow down sometimes.

Taking breaks, stepping away from my screen, and giving myself time to decompress are important parts of taking care of myself. I don’t want to become so focused on my responsibilities that I forget to protect my peace of mind.

Rest is not wasted time.

Sometimes, stepping away gives me the space I need to come back with a clearer head.

06

What am I looking forward to?

I want to enjoy the little things in life.

I’d like to explore new stores, discover new places to eat, and spend time playing video games. These activities might seem simple, but they give me something to look forward to outside of school, work, and career planning.

I want to remind myself that I don’t have to accomplish something significant every day for the day to be worthwhile.

07

What does it mean to become anything in this world while remaining humble?

To me, it means respecting people from different backgrounds and recognizing that everyone has something to teach me.

I never know how a simple act of kindness or respect might affect someone, or how our paths might cross again in the future. Being open to other people’s experiences lets me learn about them and understand perspectives beyond my own.

I want to keep growing, stay ambitious, and recognize my own worth without believing I’m above anyone else.

My closing thought

I want to keep building my future without losing myself in the process. I can pursue my career, make better financial decisions, develop meaningful friendships, and still give myself permission to rest and enjoy life.

I don’t have to choose between becoming successful and being at peace with who I am today.

01

How am I feeling today?

Today, I felt a bit drained, but I was also curious. I spent much of the day reading developer documentation and exploring ways to solve a technical problem on my own.

Learning something new can be mentally exhausting, especially when I run into obstacles I can’t resolve by myself. Even so, I wanted to understand what I was working with instead of giving up when I hit a problem.

02

What did I work on today?

I spent time learning about Tenable and vulnerability management. I learned how it helps organizations view and organize assets across their environments using information such as IP addresses, tags, and asset filters.

I was challenged to use the API developer reference to see whether I could bulk delete assets that way. It was an opportunity to learn how security tools work behind the scenes, beyond their interfaces.

03

What challenged me?

One of my biggest challenges was understanding how the API credentials needed to be formatted, including the access key and secret key. Because I’m a current intern, I also had concerns about elevated privileges. I wasn’t sure whether my account would have the access needed to run scripts through PowerShell or the API testing tools.

I worked through the PowerShell scripts in the documentation so I could learn what each section did.

I wanted to solve the problem independently, but I also recognized that I was working in a corporate environment where permissions and changes must be handled carefully. I didn’t want to risk affecting assets or making changes without the proper authorization.

I also wanted to make a good impression through my security engineering work. Sometimes, that expectation made the challenge feel more difficult than it needed to be.

Another challenge right now is SAS. I’ve been learning the SAS programming language, which is used in data science, and it’s still pretty new to me. I’m currently competing in a SAS hackathon that runs from October 1 to October 31, and succeeding in it is my top priority.

04

What am I learning about myself?

I’m learning that when I don’t know an answer, I tend to research it. In technology, there is always something new to learn, and even experienced professionals run into unfamiliar problems.

I don’t have to know everything immediately. I can search the documentation, study examples, experiment in my own lab, and gradually understand how things work.

At the same time, I need to recognize the difference between experimenting in a personal lab and working in a corporate environment. What I test on my own equipment is different from what I’m authorized to test at work.

I can be curious and independent while still knowing when to ask for help.

05

What do I want to remember?

I want to remember that permissions, responsibility, and communication are important parts of cybersecurity.

As an intern, I have certain access limitations. If I’m unsure whether I have the right permissions, it’s better to ask for clarification than to risk an unauthorized or unintended change.

I don’t have to prove myself by solving every problem alone.

Being responsible with the systems I’m trusted to work with is part of being a good security professional.

06

What do I want to do after work?

After work, I want to give myself time to rest, sleep, eat, and take care of myself. I also want to turn off my screens and spend some time away from technology.

I still have interviews to prepare for and a presentation to finish, so I may not have a completely free evening. Still, I can try to fit in a short walk and give myself a little breathing room between responsibilities.

Taking care of myself matters, even when I have several things to accomplish.

07

A thought to carry forward

When I face an unfamiliar challenge, I want to remind myself that it’s okay not to find the answer immediately.

Sometimes I’ll go down a rabbit hole while researching something, and that’s part of learning. I don’t have to understand everything in one sitting.

I want to set aside 30 minutes to an hour to reflect on what I’ve learned, without feeling obligated to spend the entire day mastering it.

There will always be something new to discover. What matters is that I stay curious, learn from the experience, and know when to take a step back.

My closing thought

I don’t have to know every answer today. I just have to keep learning, act responsibly, and give myself the time to grow.

How am I feeling today?

02

What did I learn at work?

Today, I took a deeper look into my new vulnerability management responsibilities, particularly the workflow involving Tenable, ServiceNow, and Azure DevOps.

I learned more about analyzing vulnerabilities, including the recent PHP 2 Shell vulnerability, and identifying which assets within the organization may be affected.

An important part of this process is documenting findings in ServiceNow so the appropriate teams can understand the issues, prioritize remediation, and take action.

I also reviewed remediation guidance in Azure DevOps. It helped me understand how a vulnerability’s priority can depend on factors such as whether the asset is internet-facing or internal, its severity, and its assigned baseline.

I learned that remediation timelines can vary depending on severity and the applicable requirements. I want to keep learning how these timelines are determined and how to document them accurately.

I’m beginning to appreciate how these tools work together. Vulnerability management isn’t simply about finding security weaknesses. It’s also about communicating the risks, documenting the findings, and helping the organization move toward remediation.

What did I experience outside of work?

I had an interview with a well-known nonprofit organization for its Cybersecurity New Professionals Program.

The interview lasted about an hour. They asked about my background, my previous work experience, and what I hope to accomplish in the future.

I’ve gained experience in different areas of cybersecurity, and I want to keep exploring opportunities that will help me develop my skills and become a stronger professional.

How am I connecting my education with my personal projects?

My goal is to make my GitHub more structured and easier to navigate, while documenting what I’ve learned and how I apply it outside the classroom.

I also have a management assignment to complete on competitive advantage. It’s another opportunity to apply business concepts to real-world examples and think about how organizations differentiate themselves.

I want to keep reviewing my finance notes, too. These concepts may become more useful as I deepen my understanding of business, financial decision-making, and the future of my own company.
